A tiny menu bar app that keeps your MacBook awake with the lid closed, in Low Power Mode with the display off, so AI agents and long builds keep running. Open the lid and everything goes back.

Lid open. Your agent is working. Lid closed. Still running.
Low Power Mode · display off

01

Approve once

On first launch, one admin prompt adds a sudoers rule that allows only pmset sleep and Low Power changes.

02

Close the lid

The Mac stays awake, switches to Low Power Mode and turns the display off. Your agents keep going.

03

Open it

Your exact battery and charger settings come back. Nothing to undo.

It never stays awake unattended. Heat, a low battery, quit, logout or a crash all let the Mac sleep again.

Questions

Why does it ask for my password?

Once, on first launch, to add a rule that lets Decaffeinate run a short list of pmset commands without asking again: sleep on or off, Low Power Mode on or off. Nothing else. It can't install software, read your files or run other commands as admin. The rule is one line in /etc/sudoers.d/decaffeinate, and you can read it.

macOS says it can't check the app for malicious software. Is it safe?

The warning appears because Decaffeinate isn't notarized: that needs a paid Apple developer account, and the app is free. The full source is on GitHub and every release is built by GitHub's servers from that source. To open it: click Done, then System Settings › Privacy & Security › Open Anyway. Only the first time.

What if it crashes with the lid closed?

A small background check, installed with the app, notices within a minute, turns lid-close sleep back on and puts the Mac to sleep. Your Low Power Mode settings come back too.

Will it overheat in a bag?

No. On battery, the Mac goes to sleep as soon as it runs warm, because a closed MacBook in a bag can't cool down. It also sleeps when the battery drops below 20%, or the limit you choose.

How much battery does it use?

Pilot Light's measurements on an M5 Max MacBook Pro: 4.8 W with the lid closed against 16.7 W open, which is about 20 hours instead of 6. Heavy builds draw more.

Does it send any data?

No. Without your consent it makes no network requests at all. If you turn on update checks, it asks GitHub for the latest version number, and sends nothing about your Mac.

How do I remove it?

Menu › Settings › Uninstall… It turns sleep back on, removes the rule and the background check, and moves itself to the Bin.
